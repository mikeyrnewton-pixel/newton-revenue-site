THE 30-MINUTE STARTER PROCESS

One row per person, per training requirement.

1. Decide what you actually need to track. Start with a training-needs review for each role and the relevant risk assessment. Do not assume every course has a legally mandated annual renewal. Record the source of any review/expiry date (training provider, certificate, company policy or specific regulation).

2. Download and open the CSV template. Import it into your existing spreadsheet application. The CSV contains headers only, not real staff records. Keep a stable internal employee reference instead of putting unnecessary personal information in a shared file.

3. Enter the evidence. For each requirement, record the employee reference, course or competency, completion date, review or expiry date, restricted evidence location, responsible owner and last verified date. Use real date cells, not text like “next summer”.

4. Add a visible status. In cell F2, enter the following Excel formula and copy it down. It treats an empty date as needing review; it does not invent an expiry date:

=IF(D2="","CHECK DATE",IF(D2<TODAY(),"EXPIRED",IF(D2<=TODAY()+7,"DUE 7 DAYS",IF(D2<=TODAY()+30,"DUE 30 DAYS","CURRENT"))))

If your Excel uses semicolons instead of commas, adjust the separators. The formula assumes column D contains actual spreadsheet dates. Test with one past date, one seven days away and one date more than 30 days away before relying on it.

5. Assign one weekly reviewer. Filter “EXPIRED”, “DUE 7 DAYS” and “DUE 30 DAYS”; check the source documents, decide whether retraining or a competency review is appropriate, and log the next action. Urgent competence concerns require a person to assess work allocation immediately, not a spreadsheet reminder.

6. Review access and backups. Give edit access only to people who need it. Store certificate evidence in an approved restricted location, not an open shared folder. Define retention and deletion rules; check that you can restore the file.

BEFORE YOU AUTOMATE

Where this simple version breaks.

No automatic alerts

A CSV cannot send notifications. Spreadsheet formulas only help when somebody opens and reviews the register. Calendar reminders are a separate, human-approved step.

Dates are not competence

A current certificate is not proof that someone can perform every task safely. Different roles, risks and qualifications need different reviews.

Privacy and control

Training records may be personal data. Apply UK GDPR access, purpose, retention and accuracy controls. Do not add medical records or confidential assessments to this simple tracker.

What does it cost?

The template is free. If you already have spreadsheet software, there is no new software charge for this approach. You still need time to check source documents, correct records, maintain access permissions and review exceptions. A larger multi-site team may eventually need audit logs, role-based permissions, alerts and integration with HR systems. Those requirements should be scoped and priced rather than promised for free.

What about AI?

AI might help draft a summary of missing fields or suggest questions for a reviewer, but it should not infer training validity from an ambiguous document or approve someone to work. Never upload employee certificates or health information to a third-party AI tool without appropriate data protection assessment and contractual controls.

Check the underlying guidance

The UK Health and Safety Executive says training records help identify refresher needs. Its workplace transport guidance recommends a central register with training history and needs. For asbestos work, HSE cautions that a certificate's expiry is not automatically proof that full retraining is legally required. The Information Commissioner's Office explains minimisation, security and retention for employment records. These sources are guidance, not a substitute for role-specific legal or safety advice.

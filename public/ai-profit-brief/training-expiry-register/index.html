Which staff training needs reviewing next month?

A simple, no-subscription training register for small teams: one row per person and training item, clear ownership, and optional 7- and 30-day review flags.

Written by Newton Revenue. The example data is fictional. This is a tracking aid, not compliance certification or evidence that we have deployed it for a client.

The problem worth fixing

Training records often live across certificates, inboxes and managers' spreadsheets. When nobody owns the next review date, an important refresher can be missed. But a certificate's printed expiry is not always a legal retraining deadline. The right action depends on the work, risk, relevant guidance and whether the worker remains competent. For example, the UK Health and Safety Executive notes that an asbestos training certificate is not proof of competence and that an expiry date does not necessarily require a full repeat course. Read the HSE explanation.

Build the useful version in 15 minutes

  1. Agree the training requirements first. Ask the responsible manager which roles need which training, who can assess competence, and what triggers a review. Don't invent a universal annual expiry rule.
  2. Create the register using those headings and replace the fictional example. Use one row per worker and training item. Columns A–I are: Employee ID, Training item, Completed on, Review by, Responsible manager, Evidence location, Review status, Next action, Last checked. Keep real certificates in an access-controlled location; column F can be an internal reference rather than an open link.
  3. Make column D a genuine date column. In Excel, select column D, choose Date formatting and check that a test date behaves as a date. Imported dates may appear as text depending on locale. Leave Review by blank only where the requirement genuinely needs human assessment.
  4. Add the status formula. In cell G2 paste the formula below, then fill it down. It highlights review dates that are overdue, within seven days or within 30 days. This is a planning flag, not an automatic statement that a person cannot work.
=IF(D2="","REVIEW DATE NEEDED",IF(NOT(ISNUMBER(D2)),"CHECK DATE",IF(D2<TODAY(),"OVERDUE",IF(D2<=TODAY()+7,"DUE WITHIN 7 DAYS",IF(D2<=TODAY()+30,"DUE WITHIN 30 DAYS","CURRENT")))))

Important: some Excel installations use semicolons instead of commas as formula separators. Change the separators if your version requires it. The formula updates when the workbook recalculates or opens; it does not email anybody.

  1. Give every flagged row a next action. For example: “manager to confirm refresher needed”, “book approved provider” or “review role change”. Enter the decision in column H and the last check date in I.
  2. Schedule a human review. Filter G for OVERDUE / DUE WITHIN 7 DAYS / DUE WITHIN 30 DAYS each Monday. Assign one owner. If a record is genuinely safety-critical, escalate using the employer's existing safety process rather than relying on a spreadsheet reminder.
  3. Test failure cases. Add a date in the past, tomorrow, in 20 days, a blank, and a text value such as “unknown”. Check the flags before relying on the sheet. Protect formula cells and back up the file.

Costs, privacy and limits

Software: this starter is free. You can use an existing licensed Excel installation or another spreadsheet that supports equivalent formulas; no purchase is required for this template. Human time: allow time to define training requirements, import records, verify dates and review exceptions. Scaling: once multiple people edit the same register, you may need version control, permissions, change logs and dependable notifications. This file supplies none of those automatically.

Employee data: a staff training register can contain personal information. The Information Commissioner's Office guidance explains lawful basis, purpose, data minimisation, access controls and retention for employment records. Keep only necessary fields, tell staff how records are used, limit access and set a justified retention schedule. Avoid putting medical details in this template.

Compliance: this is an administrative aid. Sector rules, qualifications, actual competence and safety-critical training decisions require appropriate human assessment. HSE also advises employers that training records can help identify refresher needs; see its training guidance.

When to automate — and when not to

If the register works for a small team, keep it simple. If you have many sites, recurring reminders or frequent staff changes, the next step may be a restricted shared register with audit trails, a weekly digest and human-approved actions. Don't send employee data to an AI provider just to produce a colour-coded list; ordinary spreadsheet formulas do that job without AI.
